# Ecommerce page: content audit and revised strategy

Route: `/services/ecommerce` · Reviewed: 25 October 2026

## Assessment

The original page had the right foundation: outcomes before capability details,
real product brands, an in-house team, a short process, and clear ways to enquire.
It needed a more balanced launch/rebuild message, earlier work, a clearer software
offer, and more precise promises. The latest revision uses descriptive headings,
shorter sentences, and plain service explanations throughout. This is a content
revision within the existing design system.

## Findings and changes

| Finding | Implemented change |
| --- | --- |
| The hero used a slogan instead of a service description. | “Custom ecommerce design and development.” names the service directly. |
| Complex products and failing stores dominated; a first-time product launch was less visible. | The hero explicitly addresses launching a product brand or rebuilding a store. The first situation now describes a product ready for launch. |
| Five projects appeared after four pain cards and six outcome cards. | Selected work now follows the proof strip, before situations and outcomes. |
| “Brought online” could imply responsibility for every part of all five client engagements. | The proof label says “Product brands we’ve worked with.” Project summaries describe the product experience without claiming full delivery or measured results. |
| The custom software offer could read as just a storefront plus an app. | The store outcome and FAQ name custom ecommerce software, storefront, admin tools, apps, and relevant integrations. No unconfirmed app count or platform name is added. |
| “Most launches need five agencies” was an unsupported comparison. | “One team for design and development” explains the delivery model directly. |
| Some outcomes sounded automatic: understanding in seconds, self-quoting orders, repeat purchases. | Headlines now describe clearer decisions, rule-based prices or quote requests, and a better buying experience. |
| The free redesign could be mistaken for a built page or whole store. | It is explicitly a free homepage or single product page design concept. |
| “Book a call” led to a contact form. | Page-specific primary CTAs now say “Discuss your project.” The closing copy tells visitors what to share. |

## Positioning

Help product companies launch or improve their digital presence with branding,
product storytelling, and custom commerce created by one in-house team. Make
products easier to understand, choose, configure, and buy. Handle creative and
technical delivery so the client can focus on their product and business.

The audience includes founders and marketing or ecommerce leaders with a defined
product line: premium consumer products, configurable products, and technical
products sold through enquiries or quotations. A direct checkout is one buying
route; a complete quote request is another.

## Section-by-section strategy

| Order | Section | Job of the content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero: “Custom ecommerce design and development.” | Name the service directly. Explain that one in-house team supports new and existing stores. Primary: “Discuss your project.” Secondary: “See the work.” |
| 2 | Proof | Name product brands and retain the previously approved studio figures. Use the work to establish expertise. |
| 3 | Selected work: “Ecommerce and product websites” | Explain each site's purpose and visible features in one sentence. |
| 4 | Situations: “For new launches and existing stores” | Describe four needs: launch a store, explain a product, simplify custom orders, and update an existing store. |
| 5 | Outcomes: “Branding, product content, and custom ecommerce” | Explain product features, selection and quotes, consistent branding, products and orders, campaign content, and support after launch. Capabilities remain short service tags. |
| 6 | One team: “One team for design and development” | Explain one point of contact, consistent design and content, and less coordination for the client. |
| 7 | Process: “How we deliver your project” | Name the stages directly: discovery, planning, design, development, and launch and support. |
| 8 | “Free homepage or product page concept” | Specify the one-page design offer and retain budget, timing, and scope information. CTA: “Request a concept.” |
| 9 | FAQ and “Tell us about your project” | Answer service, 3D, ERP, migration, timing, cost, and support questions briefly. Ask for a product/store link, goals, and target date. |

## Voice and proof rules

- Confident, specific, and direct. Use product details and the work to show experience.
- Headings name the service, topic, or task; they should make sense without the paragraph below.
- Prefer familiar words and short sentences. Describe what is included and what the customer can do with it.
- Avoid slogan pairs, rhetorical questions, simulated client quotes, and phrases such as “product storytelling” or “a considered experience.”
- Discuss the buying decision and the client's operations before naming tools.
- Keep capability explanations for their own pages; this page explains what they enable.
- Use original language. The brief's “Your product is complex. We make it obvious.”
  and its following discipline line are the [Riotters homepage copy](https://riotters.com/).
  The page keeps its own hero and replaces the earlier “made obvious” work heading.
- Do not invent conversion lifts, testimonials, or responsibility for whole client sites.
- Retain the studio figures and commercial starting points recorded as approved in
  the prior strategy: 10 years, 500+ products, 25+ specialists, five regions,
  projects from $10k, and a typical 1–3 month launch. These were not independently
  validated by this audit; complex projects need an agreed longer timeline.

Ziemi pages. The REFIRE URL could not be retrieved by the research tool; its
description stays broad and follows the existing brief. Detailed contributions
for each engagement still need approved case study material.

## Remaining content needs

1. Supply real client imagery and the hero product film. Every ecommerce media
   slot currently has no image source; the rendered page shows grey placeholders.
   Stronger copy cannot establish the visual quality without the work itself.
2. Add an approved one-line contribution to each project when available, and
   verified outcomes or a client quote where they exist. Avoid inventing results.

## Measurement

Track qualified enquiries originating from this page, requests for the free
concept, work-link clicks, and clicks on each primary CTA. Compare quality as
well as volume; quotation-led product companies may be relevant even when their
sales process does not end in an online checkout.
