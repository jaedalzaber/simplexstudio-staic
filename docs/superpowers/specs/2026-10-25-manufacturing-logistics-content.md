## Recommended name and positioning

**Solution name: Manufacturing & Logistics**

**Page heading: Custom software for manufacturing and logistics.**

This name covers manufacturing ERP, production management, warehouse and
transport workflows, digital twins, and industrial training. Keep ERP explicit
in the hero, navigation summary, and search metadata. Retain the existing
`/services/industrial-erp` route during implementation unless a separate URL
change is agreed.

**Positioning:** Custom ERP and connected industrial software, with 3D
configuration, operational visualization, and VR/AR training delivered by one
in-house team.

The main story is the connection between customer requirements and factory
operations: product choices and quotes, approved specifications, materials,
production, inspection, and dispatch. Manufacturers can also engage the team
for an internal ERP, logistics application, twin, or training system independently
of an ecommerce website.

Do not describe 3D alone as a unique advantage. Several global providers below
already combine industrial data with 3D and XR. The proposed studio positioning
is the coordinated delivery of product presentation, custom workflows, software,
and reusable visual assets, supported by the Skywings engagement.

### Conclusions for this page

1. **Lead with the business process.** Epicor demonstrates the connection from
   visual configuration to engineering/manufacturing outputs; SAP organizes
   operations around production definitions, planning, execution, and quality.
2. **Make the 3D useful.** Siemens and DELMIA explain production and material
   flows, not simply a navigable factory model.
3. **Give training its own evidence.** Capgemini, Rockwell, Tata Elxsi, and
   Program-Ace present training as a specific application of industrial assets.
4. **Keep promises specific.** The competitors span ERP, simulation, control,
   and immersive experiences. This page should state which problem a proposed
   project solves rather than implying every project includes all of them.

These are editorial recommendations drawn from the cited pages, not claims
that the studio has already delivered each competitor capability. Borrow the
clarity of the structure and demonstrations; write original copy and use the
studio's own visual identity. Do not reuse competitor metrics or testimonials.

## Page structure and proposed copy

### 1. Hero

**Eyebrow:** Manufacturing & Logistics

**H1:** Custom software for manufacturing and logistics.

**Body:** We build ERP systems that connect orders, materials, production,
and dispatch. Our in-house team creates 3D configurators, digital twins, and
VR/AR training to help customers and staff understand products and operations.

**Primary CTA:** Discuss your project

**Secondary CTA:** Explore the factory demo

**Supporting links:** Custom ERP · 3D configuration · Factory digital twins ·
Logistics software · VR/AR training

**Visual:** A small factory view paired with a readable work-order panel.
Start with a lightweight preview and keep the service description immediately
visible. Industrial software should be apparent before any demo loads.

### 2. Showcase one: Skywings

**H2:** Skywings: ecommerce, 3D products, and manufacturing ERP

**Draft caption:** For Skywings, a machinery and metalwork manufacturer,
we developed an ecommerce frontend with a 3D product viewer and configurator,
created product renders, and built ERP software for its manufacturing process.

Show three connected views with captions:

- **Customer experience:** Ecommerce interface and product configuration.
- **Product presentation:** Viewer and renders for the manufactured products.
- **Manufacturing software:** The ERP interface and the actual workflow it supports.

This delivery scope comes from the user's brief. Confirm the public brand
spelling, images, and exact ERP modules when preparing the case study. Show
the ERP at least as prominently as the storefront.

Do not claim Skywings already has automatic configuration-to-BOM transfer,
live machine monitoring, logistics modules, a digital twin, or a training
system unless those specific deliveries are confirmed. No performance figures
or public project URL have been provided.

### 3. Configuration, quoting, and production planning

**H2:** Connect product configuration to production planning

**Body:** Let customers or sales teams configure a product in 3D and request
a quote. Use the approved specification to prepare materials and production
work, based on your engineering and manufacturing rules.

Use one compact workflow rather than separate capability cards:

**3D configuration → quote → approved specification → material planning →
production order → inspection and dispatch**

**Visual:** A configured product beside a quote summary and its material list.
Use consistent product dimensions, quantities, and reference numbers across
the views so the connection is understandable.

Explain three client outcomes in short captions:

- Customers and sales teams can review product choices visually.
- Engineering receives a clear, versioned specification for review.
- Approved requirements can inform bills of materials, material availability,
  and work orders through the agreed rules and integrations.

Automatic BOM generation is a scoped feature that needs validated product
rules and engineering data. Preserve the approval/release step before work
enters production. Explain a bill of materials as the parts and quantities
needed to make the product; keep CPQ/MRP terminology secondary.

### 4. Custom manufacturing ERP

**H2:** Plan and manage production in one system

**Body:** Give planning, purchasing, and production teams a shared view of
what needs to be made, which materials are available, and how each job is
progressing. Build the software around your manufacturing process.

Show one readable dashboard with three focused views:

- **Materials and purchasing:** Parts, bills of materials, stock availability,
  supplier orders, and shortages.
- **Planning and work orders:** Production routes, work centres, schedules,
  assigned jobs, and progress.
- **Quality and traceability:** Inspection records, issue handling, and the
  history of the relevant job, batch, or serial-numbered item.

Use desktop planning and a simple tablet view for shop-floor updates. Connect
accounting, customer records, existing ERP, or other business tools where
needed. Define the modules per project rather than suggesting every engagement
replaces all business software or includes every finance/HR function.

Machinery, metal fabrication, industrial equipment, and assembled products
are useful example contexts. Describe broader manufacturing suitability as
custom development after process discovery, not prebuilt industry coverage.

### 5. Warehouse and logistics software

**H2:** Connect inventory, dispatch, and delivery

**Body:** Track materials and finished goods as they move through storage,
production, and delivery. Give warehouse and logistics teams the information
they need to prepare, dispatch, and follow each order.

Show an order moving through three operational stages:

- **Receive and store:** Goods received, stock locations, transfers, and
  material allocation.
- **Prepare and dispatch:** Picking, packing, shipment preparation, and
  dispatch status.
- **Transport and delivery:** Carrier or fleet assignment, shipment updates,
  and delivery records where required.

**Visual:** A warehouse zone or rack selected in 3D beside a stock/location
panel, followed by a shipment status view. A simplified map can support the
delivery view; do not use animated vehicle dots as proof of live tracking.

3D can help staff locate a bin, staging area, or shipment. Barcode scanning,
carrier integrations, driver apps, GPS tracking, and route planning are scoped
modules. Warehouse records and transport data need the appropriate source
connections. This is software delivery, not a freight-handling service.

### 6. Factory digital twin demo

**H2:** Explore production in a factory digital twin

**Body:** Select a work area to review its jobs, material requirements, and
production status. Follow a sample order from raw materials to dispatch.

**Visible label:** Factory digital twin demo · Simulated data

Use an illustrative metalwork factory, separate from Skywings. Show six areas:
materials, cutting, fabrication, assembly, inspection, and dispatch.

The proposed interaction is detailed below. The demonstration should explain

### 7. Showcase two: illustrative industrial training

**H2:** Train teams with VR and AR

**Body:** Create training around your equipment, workplace, and approved
procedures. Let people practise tasks in VR, or use AR guidance to understand
components and follow instructions in context.

**Showcase title:** Industrial machine assembly and inspection

**Visible label:** Illustrative training example

Show a short learning sequence: identify components → follow a guided virtual
assembly → inspect the result → review feedback. Pair a VR preview with a
trainer dashboard showing completed steps, attempts, and assessment results.

Use an optional tablet AR view to demonstrate component labels or approved

Describe onboarding, equipment familiarisation, assembly, inspection, and
maintenance practice as possible applications. Training content and assessment

The user explicitly selected an illustrative example for this second showcase.
Do not invent a client name, testimonial, project result, certification claim,
or reduction in incidents/training time. A browser preview is not evidence
of an implemented headset deployment.

**Body:** Our designers, 3D artists, and developers work together on the

### 9. Questions and project enquiry

**H2:** Discuss your manufacturing or logistics project

**Body:** Tell us what you make or move, how your team works today, and where
you need better software or visibility. We’ll discuss the workflows, data,
visuals, and integrations your project needs.

**CTA:** Discuss your project

Use five concise FAQs:

- **Can you build an ERP without a new ecommerce website?** Yes. The scope
  can focus on internal manufacturing or logistics operations independently.
- **Can the software work with our existing ERP and equipment?** Review
  the available APIs, records, devices, and data connections before agreeing
  the integration scope.
- **Can configuration create material requirements?** Yes, with validated
  configuration rules, engineering data, and the agreed approval process.
- **Does a digital twin need live sensor data?** Its connections depend on
  the purpose: operational records can support job visibility, while machine
  telemetry needs an appropriate equipment or sensor connection.
- **Can we start with one process or training module?** Yes. Agree a pilot,
  validate it with users, and plan the next stages from the results.

Discuss budget and timing against scope; none is supplied in the brief.

## Factory demo: practical interaction plan

1. **Open the factory.** Start from a fast static preview. Load the 3D scene
   on request; provide a 2D overview and list with equivalent selections.
2. **Select a work area.** Highlight the selected area and show its identifier,
   current sample jobs, material readiness, stage, and next step.
3. **Follow one order.** Use a single sample fabricated equipment enclosure
   with a stable order number. Highlight its route through the six areas and
   show the approved specification and parts summary when relevant.
4. **Review an exception.** Switch between normal progress and a material
   shortage. In the shortage scenario, the affected job waits for material;
   downstream readiness changes consistently. Explain the reason in text.
5. **Compare visual and list views.** Keep the same order, selected area,
   and scenario in both views. Offer reset and explicit camera controls.

Use status labels as well as colour. Keep graphics stylised and recognisable,
with restrained lighting and limited animation; avoid an entire industrial city.
Keep page scrolling usable on touch and provide reduced-motion support.

The sample records drive the view; numbers and statuses should follow the
scenario rather than change randomly. Do not present invented throughput,
OEE, delivery estimates, or cost savings as measured results. This demo is
not a validated process simulator or a machine-control interface.

For a real twin, identify the assets, linked ERP/MES/warehouse/equipment data,
update frequency, and intended decisions. A 3D factory scene without a data
connection is a visual model. The site's sample demonstrates that connection
with simulated records, not a live client factory.

## Design, content, and preparation

Follow the existing solution pages: shared typography, sky hero, blue accents,
rounded media, pill CTAs, and restrained motion. Give the software interfaces
enough space to be readable. Use a consistent sample product and visual system
across the configuration, ERP, warehouse, and factory demo views.

Keep headings descriptive and each public content block to one or two short
sentences. Maintain a clear distinction between delivered work, offered custom
scope, and illustrative demonstrations. The research is internal planning
context; competitor logos and comparisons do not need to appear on the page.

Prepare these assets for implementation:

- Skywings product, storefront, configurator, rendering, and ERP media, with
  accurate contribution captions and the confirmed public brand spelling.
- Sample product specifications, parts, work-order route, and coherent demo data.
- A small factory asset set and warehouse location view.
- The illustrative training storyboard, VR preview, and trainer interface.
- The actual modules, data connections, deployment needs, and support scope
  appropriate to the first implementation.
