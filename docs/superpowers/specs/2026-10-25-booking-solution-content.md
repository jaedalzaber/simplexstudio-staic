# Booking Systems: solution page content strategy

Prepared: 25 October 2026

Status: implemented at `/services/booking-system` (see
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-25-booking-solution-page.md`). Selected work
stays hidden until approved projects are supplied. Interface previews were
recreated from this brief because images 1–11 are not in the repository yet.

## Name and positioning

**Solution name: Booking Systems**

Keep the existing `/services/booking-system` route when implementing. Use a
descriptive heading to explain the broader offer.

**Positioning:** Custom booking software, websites, apps, and visual space
selection, delivered by one in-house team. Help customers make a booking and
give the business the tools to manage it.

The page should establish three things early:

- The booking flow is adapted to the business: appointments, reservations,
  spaces, or seats.
- Customer-facing experiences connect with scheduling, customer records,
  custom forms, and staff tools.
- 2D and 3D selection make the location and layout clear when customers need
  to choose a physical space.

Present this as a tailored service using existing software and custom work.
Keep the underlying product unnamed. Avoid suggesting every module is built
from scratch or that every industry has a finished, identical system.

## Section-by-section content

### 1. Hero

**Eyebrow:** Booking Systems

**H1:** Custom booking systems for services and spaces.

**Body:** We customise booking software and create the websites, apps, and
visual booking experiences around it. Help customers choose a service, time,
or space, and give your team clear tools to manage each booking.

**Primary CTA:** Discuss your project

**Secondary CTA:** Try the booking demo

**Supporting links:** Appointments and reservations · 2D and 3D selection ·
Customer management · Websites and apps

**Visual:** One coherent restaurant booking example: a room preview beside
its reservation panel, with a smaller staff schedule behind it. Use a static
preview here; put the interactive experience in its own section.

### 2. Selected work

**H2:** Booking and service business projects

Show two or three approved projects, each with a clear image and one sentence:
what the business needed and the specific part delivered. Prefer a mix of
customer booking, business website, and management interface.

Client projects have not yet been supplied for this page. Add this section
when the work and contribution details are available. A service website can
demonstrate website design without being presented as a booking-system project.
Keep the restaurant demo separate from client work.

### 3. Booking options for different businesses

**H2:** Booking flows built around your business

**Body:** The booking rules depend on what customers are reserving. We adapt
the steps, availability, and management tools to your services and operations.

Use six compact examples, rather than six detailed service sections:

| Business | What customers choose |
| --- | --- |
| Hotels and stays | Dates, guests, and an available room or room type. |
| Workspaces | A desk, meeting room, or workspace for a chosen time. |
| Restaurants | Party size, time, and a suitable table or seating area. |
| Clinics and dental practices | A service, practitioner, and appointment time. |
| Professional services | A service, staff member, or consultation slot. |
| Transport | A route, departure, and available seat. |

These illustrate possible project scopes. Confirm each industry's rules and
integrations during planning. Use maps and 3D where location matters; use a
clear service and calendar flow where that is more helpful.

### 4. Inline restaurant demo

**H2:** Choose a table in 2D or 3D

**Body:** Explore the restaurant layout, check sample availability, and select
a table for your group. Switch between a floor plan and a 3D view as you choose.

**Demo flow:** Guests, date, and time → available tables → table details →
preview reservation.

Keep the experience small and focused:

- One furnished restaurant room with around 8–12 tables, recognisable seating
  areas, soft lighting, and a clear cutaway view.
- A 2D/3D toggle showing the same tables and selection in both views. Load
  the 3D scene on request so the page stays fast.
- Select a table or seating area, with seat capacity shown in the details.
  Do not make every chair a separate booking target for this example.
- Show table number, capacity, location, and the selected time. Update the
  sample availability when the time or party size changes.
- Clearly mark available, unavailable, and selected tables. Provide an
  equivalent table list for mobile, keyboard access, and 3D fallback.
- End with a sample confirmation. Collect no personal details or payment.

**Visible disclosure:** Interactive demo with sample availability. No real
reservation is made.

Use a digital-twin visual style, but describe it publicly as an interactive
venue layout. A real deployment can connect the layout to the booking system;
the demo does not demonstrate live venue data. Explain that the same selection
approach can be adapted to desks, rooms, and transport seats.

### 5. Booking management and customer records

**H2:** Manage bookings and customers in one place

**Body:** Give your team a clear view of bookings, availability, and customer
details. Adapt the dashboard to the people, spaces, and services you manage.

Show three focused interface views with short captions:

- **Booking calendar:** Review appointments or reservations by date, staff
  member, or resource, according to the agreed setup.
- **Customer records:** Keep contact details, booking history, and submitted
  information together for follow-up.
- **Availability and resources:** Manage opening hours, service duration,
  staff schedules, and capacity as required by the business.

Use the supplied software as the basis for real interface previews. Present
custom modules as project options. Payments, deposits, reminders, calendar
connections, reporting, and other integrations belong in the agreed scope;
do not present them as universally connected features.

### 6. Custom forms and business websites

**H2:** Custom booking forms and business websites

**Body:** Create booking forms and pages that fit your services and brand.
Connect the information customers submit with the tools your team uses.

Use a two-part visual sequence:

- **Form builder:** Show a visual editor beside a finished booking form.
  Demonstrate service choices, custom questions, date and time, and customer
  details. Explain how the form collects the information needed for the booking.
- **Website and page builder:** Show a polished service page and its editing
  interface. Make clear that the team can design a new website, improve an
  existing brand presence, and provide editable pages for ongoing updates.

Use one consistent example business across the website, form, and dashboard.
Keep branding or a brand refresh as part of the service when needed, rather
than adding a long capability section. Show confirmation and follow-up as part
of the booking journey, not as unrelated form submissions.

### 7. Apps and on-site booking

**H2:** Booking across web, mobile, and on-site screens

**Body:** Extend the same booking process to the places your customers and
staff use it. Add customer apps, staff tools, or on-site booking screens where
they support your operation.

Show three concise examples:

- **Customer apps:** Browse services or spaces, book, and review reservations.
- **Staff tools:** View the schedule and update booking status.
- **On-site screens:** A self-service booking or check-in screen, or a display
  showing room and slot availability.

Use a compact website/phone/display composition. Distinguish an interactive
kiosk from an information display. Confirm hardware, connectivity, and data
connections per project; native apps are an option rather than a default bundle.

### 8. In-house delivery and support

**H2:** One team for design, 3D, and software

**Body:** Our designers, 3D artists, and developers work together on your
website, booking experience, and management tools. You have one point of
contact from planning through launch and ongoing support.

Use one compact process row:

1. **Plan:** Map booking rules, customer steps, staff workflows, and integrations.
2. **Design:** Develop the interfaces, brand assets, and venue visuals needed.
3. **Build:** Customise the software and connect the agreed channels and tools.
4. **Launch and support:** Test the booking flow, prepare the team, and support updates.

Use short capability links only. This section explains coordinated delivery;
the capability pages can explain modelling, rendering, UI design, and development.

### 9. Questions and project enquiry

**H2:** Discuss your booking project

**Body:** Tell us what customers need to book, how your team manages it today,
and which website, app, or on-site tools you need. We’ll discuss the scope,
integrations, budget, and launch plan.

**CTA:** Discuss your project

Link to the existing contact page. Avoid “Book now” as the studio enquiry CTA;
reserve booking language for the example customer interfaces.

Keep five FAQs brief:

- **Do we need 3D booking?** A calendar or 2D plan may be sufficient. Recommend
  3D when the layout, location, or setting helps customers choose.
- **Can you adapt the system to our process?** Review services, resources,
  booking rules, forms, and staff roles, then agree the customisation required.
- **Can it work with our existing website and tools?** Review the website and
  available integrations before agreeing the connection approach.
- **Can we add apps or on-site screens later?** Plan these as optional phases
  where the software, devices, and operating needs support them.
- **What will it cost and how long will it take?** Base the estimate on the
  booking rules, channels, visuals, customisation, and integrations required.

## Visual direction and supplied references

- Follow the existing solution pages and current design tokens: sky hero,
  shared typography, blue accents, rounded media, pill CTAs, and restrained motion.
- Images 1–7 show the existing forms and page-building foundation. Replace
  internal branding, unrelated imagery, placeholder content, and example customer
  data with a coherent booking scenario before using public previews.
- Images 8–11 guide interface presentation and spatial navigation. They are
  visual references, not studio work or evidence of implemented features.
- Show the restaurant plan/3D view early, then give the management interface
  and visual builders equal prominence. Use large, readable views rather than
  a montage of tiny dashboards.
- Keep the 3D scene contained in the page. Preserve page scrolling on touch,
  provide explicit camera controls, and respect reduced motion.

## Copy, proof, and launch priorities

Keep headings literal and body copy to one or two short sentences per block.
Explain what the customer or staff member can do before naming capabilities.
Avoid vague slogans, guaranteed results, invented figures, and unverified
claims of live integrations or industry-specific readiness.

For implementation, prepare: the restaurant sample layout; consistent calendar,
customer-record, form-builder, and website previews; two or three client examples
with contribution captions; and the modules available for each proposed scope.
No budget, fixed timeline, testimonial, or client claim is supplied in this plan.
